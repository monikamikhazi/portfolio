Enterprise UX Research

Making API products easier to explain

Account managers already understood their clients. What they lacked was the confidence and language to connect technical capabilities to business value. A one-week discovery sprint showed what was getting in the way.

UX Researcher · 1 week · With a UX designer, product designer and technical writer · Survey, interviews, usability testing

Helping account managers talk about APIs

The company had invested heavily in its API ecosystem, but these products rarely surfaced in customer conversations. While account managers excelled at understanding client needs, they often lacked the confidence and language to introduce technical solutions. API discussions typically happened only after customers asked for them, so the company relied on a small group of technical specialists.

We set out to help account managers link technical capabilities to the business outcomes their clients care about.

My role

As the UX researcher, I planned a mixed-methods study that fit a five-day sprint, trained the designers to run interviews alongside me so the whole team heard customers firsthand, and mentored one designer through their first sessions.

Turning research into a validated concept

We had five days to understand the problem, shape a concept, and test it with users. We kept everything in a shared Miro board, so research and design decisions developed side by side.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Clustered observations as interviews happened, so the team worked from the same evidence all week.

  2. 02

    Synthesise

    Found two mental models: relationship-focused account managers and systems-focused specialists.

  3. 03

    Frame

    Turned findings into "How might we" questions and prioritised them together.

  4. 04

    Validate

    Sketched concepts, mapped flows, and tested the prototype in five usability sessions.

Designing an AI-assisted content workflow

Research informed both the content strategy and the AI workflow: account managers described the customer challenge and selected the APIs used, AI generated a draft focused on business outcomes, and users refined it before publishing. The workflow reduced the effort of documenting success stories while keeping the language aligned with how customers understand value.

The workflow bridges the two mindsets we found: account managers start from the customer's challenge, the technical detail comes in through the APIs they select, and AI translates it into business language they can review before sharing. Each story also comes in two formats, one for each mindset: a one-pager with the benefits for relationship-focused account managers, and a process map for systems-focused specialists.

What happened next

Built and in use

The workflow was built and is live on the client's developer portal. The site admin and product owner use it to create business-focused documentation, turning technical API details into content written in customer terms.

The research changed what was built

Instead of training account managers on technical detail, the team invested in customer-ready content they could rely on, and validated it with users before development began.

16 Survey responses
5 Interviews
5 Usability tests

Key takeaway

We started by assuming the barrier was knowledge. Research showed it was confidence.

Confidence came from seeing the content, being able to review it, and trusting that what reached customers was accurate and relevant.

Research & Assessment Design

Making portal maturity measurable

Enterprise clients had invested heavily in developer portals but had no way to know if they were working, or what to build next.

UX Researcher · 6+ months · With an information architect and leadership · Model review, interviews, card sorting

Beyond interface reviews

Enterprise clients had invested heavily in developer portals but had no reliable way to evaluate their effectiveness or prioritise improvements. Existing assessments focused on interface quality, not overall portal maturity.

Internally, every evaluation relied on expert judgement, making assessments difficult to compare or scale.

We needed a framework that connected UX, technical capability and business value into one measurable definition of portal maturity.

Building an adaptable evaluation framework

Before defining portal maturity, I reviewed existing models and interviewed developers using enterprise portals.

Interviews showed that developers experience a portal as more than a website. Documentation, APIs, onboarding, governance and business goals all shape it, so organisations can choose which dimensions matter for their portal.

  1. 01

    Research

    Reviewed existing maturity models, interviewed developers, and synthesised patterns through card sorting.

  2. 02

    Framework design

    Built an evaluation framework informed by research and real-world portal use.

  3. 03

    Validation

    Tested the framework against existing customer portals.

  4. 04

    Communication

    Created executive-ready data visualisations alongside detailed engineering views for different stakeholders.

  5. 05

    Automation

    Designed an AI-assisted workflow using NotebookLM and Gemini that cut evaluation time by 80% while keeping scores consistent.

Executive level view

Different stakeholder needs influenced how results were presented.

Engineering level breakdown

Created assessment outputs tailored to product management and engineering to show a more granular view of results.

AI-assisted assessment

Instead of producing static UX audits, the assessment produced recommendations based on the dimensions each organisation chose.

Using NotebookLM and Gemini, I designed an AI-assisted workflow that automated analysis, scoring, and reporting. Manual review remained an essential step to ensure recommendations were accurate and trustworthy.

What changed

For clients

  • Clear understanding of current maturity
  • Recommendations focused on the dimensions each client chose
  • Better prioritisation of future investments
  • Executive-ready reports supporting stakeholder conversations

For the business

  • Reduced evaluation time by 80%
  • Enabled benchmarking across enterprise clients
  • Supported sales conversations with evidence rather than assumptions
  • Showed common gaps across client portals

Maturity depends on purpose

Not every part of a developer portal matters equally to every organisation.

So instead of scoring every portal against the same checklist, organisations could choose the dimensions most relevant to their goals, and the assessment focused on those.
